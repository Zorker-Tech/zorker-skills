# Page blueprints

## Global page contract

Every public page blueprint must specify:

- intent, audience, awareness stage, and success event;
- primary and secondary actions;
- section order and the question each section answers;
- required claims, proof, media, interactions, and data dependencies;
- empty, loading, error, reduced-motion, narrow-screen, and no-script behavior;
- metadata, structured data, analytics, ownership, and review trigger.

## Homepage

Recommended sequence:

1. concise category, outcome, mechanism, and primary action;
2. immediate product behavior or interactive demonstration;
3. role or job-based paths;
4. three to five capability pillars tied to outcomes;
5. credible proof near the corresponding claims;
6. enterprise control, deployment, or trust summary;
7. learning path for visitors not ready to convert;
8. final action with expectation-setting microcopy.

The homepage should route, prove, and frame. It should not attempt to contain
the full detail of every product or audience.

## Product overview

Explain the product system, main jobs, how parts relate, who owns each task,
deployment boundaries, and where to go deeper. Include a representative flow
rather than a feature grid alone.

## Product or capability detail

Use: job and outcome -> mechanism -> workflow -> controls -> examples -> proof
-> limitations or prerequisites -> integration/deployment -> action.

## Solution or use-case page

Use: recognizable operating problem -> current friction -> new workflow -> role
changes -> product map -> evidence -> adoption steps -> related learning ->
action. Reuse product facts, but make workflow, vocabulary, objections, and
proof specific to the task.

## Industry page

Publish only when the industry changes regulation, data boundaries, workflow,
stakeholders, terminology, or proof. Otherwise route through job-based pages.

## Customer evidence

Include context, starting condition, implementation, time period, measured
change, methodology, constraints, and approved attribution. If a result cannot
be substantiated, replace it with a process story or labeled example.

## Pricing or engagement page

State the commercial model, included value, meaningful usage dimensions,
eligibility, procurement path, support expectations, and what happens after the
action. When exact prices cannot be published, explain the pricing logic and
inputs rather than presenting an empty contact wall.

## Trust, security, and governance

Separate clear summaries from detailed evidence. Cover data handling,
retention, access, deployment boundaries, administrative controls, evaluation,
incident processes, user responsibilities, and evidence access. Never imply a
certification or guarantee without approval.

## Documentation entry

Route by task and starting point. Provide quick starts, concepts, examples,
reference, limits, errors, changelog, support, and migration guidance. Preserve
stable anchors and make code and prose accessible.

## Resource hub and article

The hub needs task-oriented filters, meaningful summaries, deterministic URLs,
and related paths. Articles need a direct answer, table of contents when long,
examples, definitions, freshness metadata, author or owner, and an appropriate
next action.

## Research or evaluation page

State the question, method, assumptions, dataset or environment boundaries,
results, limitations, interpretation, and reproducibility path. Separate
measured findings from opinion and product messaging.

## Company, careers, and contact

Company pages should explain mission, operating principles, leadership or
governance, and durable context. Careers pages need role reality, process,
location, accessibility, and candidate expectations. Contact flows should ask
only what is needed to route the inquiry and should explain response timing.

## Utility pages

Design search, not-found, status, legal, preference, sign-in, and error pages as
recovery experiences. They require useful next steps, accessible state
messaging, and separate analytics from conversion pages.

## Product demonstration grammar

A credible demo shows:

1. **Input** — the task, data, or instruction;
2. **Plan** — how the system interprets or decomposes work;
3. **Action** — observable steps and tool boundaries;
4. **Control** — approval, editing, constraints, or interruption;
5. **Output** — a concrete artifact or decision;
6. **Value** — a verified or explicitly illustrative result.

Provide static fallbacks and captions. Never use a looping animation as the
only explanation of capability.
