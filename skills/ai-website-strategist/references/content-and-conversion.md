# Content and conversion

## Write for decision speed

Lead with the direct answer, then add mechanism, evidence, boundaries, and
detail. A visitor should understand the page's category, value, intended user,
and next action from the first screen without decoding slogans.

Use concrete verbs and nouns. Replace inflated adjectives with observable
behavior. Distinguish what the system does, what the user does, what requires
configuration, and what remains outside scope.

## Headline and section system

A useful headline states an outcome or job. The supporting sentence adds the
audience, mechanism, or boundary. Each subsequent section should answer one
question:

1. Is this for my situation?
2. How does it work?
3. Can I control it?
4. Has it worked under relevant conditions?
5. What must I provide or change?
6. What happens after I act?

Avoid repeating the same promise in new words. Move from claim to explanation
to evidence to decision.

## Calls to action

Select actions by buying motion and readiness:

| Readiness | Useful action |
|---|---|
| exploring | view example, read guide, understand approach |
| evaluating | try workflow, inspect documentation, review trust details |
| deciding | compare plans, estimate fit, request consultation |
| ready | start, create workspace, request access |

Use one primary action per page. Button labels should describe the result, not
generic movement. Supporting microcopy should clarify cost, commitment, time,
eligibility, data required, and what happens next.

## Objection architecture

Map objections to the page best equipped to resolve them:

- relevance -> solution or use-case page;
- capability -> product detail or demonstration;
- effort -> implementation or onboarding content;
- reliability -> evaluation, customer evidence, and limitations;
- security -> trust pages and technical evidence;
- control -> workflow, permissions, review, and recovery;
- cost -> pricing logic and value model;
- vendor durability -> company, support, and operating model.

Do not bury the highest-risk objection in a frequently asked questions block.

## Proof patterns

- **Product proof**: reproducible example with inputs, outputs, and constraints.
- **Outcome proof**: baseline, change, period, method, and relevant context.
- **Technical proof**: architecture, evaluation, limitations, or reliability.
- **Operational proof**: support, deployment, control, governance, or process.
- **Adoption proof**: implementation sequence, responsibilities, and time to
  first meaningful outcome.

Place proof next to its claim. A logo wall may support familiarity but cannot
substitute for explained evidence.

## Editorial system

Plan content as clusters around durable audience questions. Each cluster needs
a canonical overview, focused supporting articles, examples, definitions,
related product paths, and a conversion appropriate to intent. Assign an owner,
review cadence, freshness trigger, and retirement rule.

## Localization

- Internationalize content models and components, not just visible strings.
- Allow expansion in navigation, headings, buttons, tables, and diagrams.
- Localize evidence, legal statements, units, dates, currencies, and actions.
- Preserve meaning and reading order across scripts and bidirectional layouts.
- Do not publish a locale whose conversion, support, or legal path is broken.
